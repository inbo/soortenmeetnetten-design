# Abstract {-}

The species monitoring programme [meetnetten.be](https://www.meetnetten.be/) aims at collecting reliable information on priority spe‐
cies in Flanders. It consists of a series of monitoring schemes which were designed by the Research
Institute for Nature and Forest (INBO). Species are counted by volunteers in a standardized way under
the coordination of the NGO Natuurpunt Studie.

Within the species monitoring programme, two mollusc species are monitored: the narrow-mouthed whorl snail (*Vertigo angustior*) and the marsh whorl snail (*Vertigo moulinsiana*).
Both are European priority species listed in Annex II of the Habitats Directive.

During the first phase of the species monitoring programme, the distribution of the narrow-mouthed whorl snail and the marsh whorl snail was systematically mapped through a targeted survey effort.

In the second phase, monitoring schemes were developed for both species with the aim of monitoring trends in abundance. This report describes the monitoring protocol for the narrow-mouthed whorl snail and the marsh whorl snail monitoring schemes, including:

- the field protocol for measuring a sampling unit,
- the development of the sampling frame,
- the sampling design and selection of sampling units within the monitoring scheme, and
- data entry in the online data portal at www.meetnetten.be.

For both monitoring schemes, the sampling frame consists of 250 × 250 m grid cells in which the target species has previously been recorded or where there is a high probability of occurrence based on the presence of suitable habitat. From this sampling frame, 60 grid cells are selected using a spatially balanced sampling design. The selected grid cells are surveyed over a six-year monitoring cycle, with 10 grid cells counted each year.

Within each selected grid cell, five promising sampling locations are identified based on a map of suitable habitat. At each sampling location, individuals of the narrow-mouthed whorl snail or the marsh whorl snail are counted during a 30-minute survey within a circular plot with a radius of 5 m.

For the narrow-mouthed whorl snail, moss, litter, and the upper soil layer are collected and sieved. The fraction between 0.5 mm and 2 mm is retained in a sorting tray and carefully examined for the presence of the species.

The marsh whorl snail is surveyed by visually inspecting living and dead leaves within vegetation that forms part of the suitable habitat.

For each sampling location, observers record the numbers of living adults, living juveniles, and (sub)fossil shells in the online data portal (www.meetnetten.be).

The first monitoring cycle runs from 2024 to 2029 for the nNarrow-mouthed whorl snail and from 2025 to 2030 for the marsh whorl snail.
